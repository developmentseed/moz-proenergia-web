# About the Platform

Mozambique has made significant progress in expanding electricity access, reaching over 60% of the population by the end of 2024\. Achieving the national goal of universal access to electricity by 2030, in line with **Sustainable Development Goal 7 (SDG7) and the National Electrification Strategy (NES)**, will require accelerated planning, investment, and coordination.

Under this premise, the Government of Mozambique, through the **Ministry of Mineral Resources and Energy (MIREME)**, has developed the **Integrated Energy Planning Platform for Mozambique**. This platform was developed under the leadership of the **Unidade Integrada de Planificação e Coordenação de Electrificação (UIPCE),** which is responsible for coordinating electrification efforts in the country, with the support of the ProEnergia Plus project led by the World Bank Group.

The platform provides access to national energy access planning data and analysis for the country, bringing together multiple models and datasets into a single, publicly accessible interface. It builds on the **Geospatial Least-Cost Electrification Pathway**, while also integrating complementary analytical models (social infrastructure, clean cooking, and productive use of electricity) and sector data to support a more comprehensive understanding of electrification opportunities, infrastructure needs, and development potential across the country.

## What the Platform Includes

The platform combines geospatial modelling, sector and settlement data with project-level information to provide a holistic view of the energy access landscape.

It includes:

* **National Electrification Pathway** – A formally adopted national planning instrument that identifies the recommended least-cost electrification technology for each settlement, using geospatial analysis.
* **Project Development & Coordination Hub** – An operational tool that tracks project pipelines, implementation progress, and investment opportunities to align both grid and off-grid electrification projects with national planning.
* Additional sectoral and contextual data to enrich project planning and analysis: 
  * **Social Infrastructure Electrification** – assessing electrification needs and investment requirements for health and education facilities.
  * **Productive Use of Energy (PUE)** – highlighting opportunities for local economic development and income generation.
  * **Clean Cooking Deployment Pathways** – analysing cost-effective solutions for expanding access to clean cooking technologies in alignment with sectoral objectives and strategy.

These components allow users to move beyond planning scenarios and explore how energy access interacts with service delivery, local economies, infrastructure, and livelihoods.

## Purpose and Use

This platform is designed as a **planning, coordination, and investment support tool**.

Users can:

* Explore electrification scenarios and model outputs  
* Identify priority areas and investment opportunities  
* Compare locations based on technical, economic, and social indicators  
* Assess development potential, including productive use opportunities  
* Track project pipelines and sector progress

By consolidating data from multiple sources, the platform supports alignment across government institutions, developers, financiers, and development partners. No single technology or actor can deliver universal access alone. A coordinated, data-driven approach is essential.

Achieving universal electrification will require significant investment by 2030, alongside strong coordination and sustained effort. The platform supports this transition by:

* Highlighting high-impact opportunities for investment  
* Linking planning results with real-world project data  
* Providing visibility on implementation progress

**Important Note:** The results presented on this platform are based on modelling assumptions and available data. They are intended to support strategic planning and decision-making, and should not be interpreted as definitive investment prescriptions for specific sites. The data presented in this platform does not constitute a substitute for field visits and site-specific project studies and should under no circumstances be interpreted as definitive investment prescriptions for specific sites.

## Institutional Leadership & Coordination

The Geospatial Least-Cost Electrification Pathway and this platform were developed under the leadership of the **UIPCE** within the **Ministry of Mineral Resources and Energy (MIREME)**, with financial support from the **World Bank Group** (through the ProEnergia Plus Project).

Technical development of the platform was led by **Development Seed**, with technical support around the structure, data, and models from **Sustainable Energy for All (SEforALL)**.

The work was carried out in close collaboration with national institutions, development partners, and private sector stakeholders, ensuring that the analysis reflects national priorities and sector realities.

## Feedback and Data Updates & Requests

This platform is continuously evolving as new data and insights become available.

The underlying data and code are publicly available via the project’s GitHub repository → [https://github.com/Mozambique-IEP](https://github.com/Mozambique-IEP).

If you identify missing, outdated, or incorrect information, please [contact the uipce.geral@mireme.gove.mz](mailto:uipce.geral@mireme.gove.mz)  to contribute updates and help improve the accuracy and relevance of the platform. 

The UIPCE supports the active use and dissemination of planning data by government entities, development partners, civil society, the private sector, and academia. If you wish to obtain data extracted from the platform for desktop analysis, please [contact the UIPCE team](mailto:uipce.geral@mireme.gove.mz) to submit a data request.
